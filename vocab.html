Study tips
01Start with a root

One root unlocks many words. Learn that cred- means "believe," and credible, incredulous, and credulous stop being three separate words.

02Learn meaning families

Most hard words are fancy ways to say something simple. Loquacious, garrulous, and verbose all mean "talkative." On the test, the simple word is often the answer.

03Sort by charge

Even when you can't define a word, knowing it is positive or negative lets you cross off choices. The charge drill trains that instinct.

04Cross the groups

Benevolent sits in the root group bene- and the family "kind." Meeting a word from two directions makes it stick.

05Learn from sentences

Read the example on the back of each card. "The tension was palpable" sticks better than a definition alone.

06Short sessions, then mix

Mark cards honestly: no clue, still learning, or know it. Review the first two piles often, and in the last weeks, quiz across all groups the way the test mixes them.
